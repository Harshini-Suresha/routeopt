What RouteOpt does

Route computation

Locations are vertices and routes are weighted edges. The system computes shortest paths and discovers routes across large map data.

Graph traversal

BFS and DFS handle connectivity checks, cycle detection and reachability queries. Topological sorting orders dependency-aware directed graphs.

Location search

A Trie powers prefix autocomplete, and a hash table gives constant-time lookup of a location and its metadata. Both are running in the search box above.

Benchmarking

A benchmarking framework compares traversal and shortest-path strategies as graphs grow, measuring runtime, memory use and scalability.

Shortest-path showdown

Dijkstra, A*, Bellman–Ford and BFS race on the same map and start–destination pair. Watch nodes visited, edges checked and route cost update live.

Map analytics

Heatmaps of visits, the cost matrix, busiest roads and scaling curves reveal how the map behaves — with every dataset downloadable as CSV.

Bellman–FordO(V · E)Shortest paths that also tolerate negative weights
BFS / DFSO(V + E)Traversal, connectivity, cycle detection, reachability

Data

The demo map is generated from seed 11 (6 × 6 grid, 36 locations, 65 roads; switch to 8 × 8 or 10 × 10 above). The same data is checked into the repo so you can reproduce every chart:

  • nodes.csv6 × 6 map: 36 locations (id, label, x, y)
  • edges.csv6 × 6 map: 65 roads (endpoints, labels, weight)
  • benchmark_allpairs.csv6 × 6 map: 400 rows, 4 algorithms × 100 random pairs
  • nodes_8x8.csv8 × 8 map: 64 locations (id, label, x, y)
  • edges_8x8.csv8 × 8 map: 121 roads
  • benchmark_8x8.csv8 × 8 map: 1,200 rows, 4 algorithms × 300 random pairs
  • scaling_benchmark.csvMeasured scaling: corner-to-corner on 25–900 node grids, median of 3 runs

Regenerate them any time with routeopt.py: make_grid_graph(6, 6, 11) + pair_effort(g, 100). The notebook walks through this step by step, and the Streamlit app lets you rebuild the map with different seeds and sizes.
